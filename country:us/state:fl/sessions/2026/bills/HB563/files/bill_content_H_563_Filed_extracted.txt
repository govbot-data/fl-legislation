19 each year as "Parkman-Lewis Day"; providing an

26 Section 2. Section 381.996, Florida Statutes, is created
27 to read:
28 381.996 Repetitive head impact prevention.—
29 (1) The Legislature finds that:
30 (a) Repetitive head impacts are inherent in high-risk
31 sports and are the root cause of chronic traumatic
32 encephalopathy.
33 (b) Youth under 18 years of age are especially vulnerable
34 due to ongoing brain development.
35 (c) Families, coaches, and even medical professionals
36 often lack information and training on repetitive head impacts
37 and chronic traumatic encephalopathy.
38 (d) Education, awareness, and informed consent are
39 necessary to protect children, reduce health and social costs,
40 and honor the memories of Mac Parkman and Ray Lewis III.
41 (2) As used in this section, the term:
42 (a) "Brain-safe sports" means sports that minimize the
43 risk of head injuries, including, but not limited to,
44 concussions and long-term brain damage such as chronic traumatic
45 encephalopathy. The term includes basketball, baseball,
46 volleyball, track, swimming, and tennis.
47 (b) "Department" means the Department of Health.
48 (c) "High-risk sports" means a sport in which a player is
49 routinely subject to repetitive head impacts and includes
50 football, soccer, hockey, boxing, lacrosse, wrestling, rugby,
